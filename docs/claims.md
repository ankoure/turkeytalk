# Claims review: what the talk says about hunting rules, checked

Every hunting rule the deck and speaker notes state, checked against its source in November 2026. Each claim gets a verdict, the source and, where needed, suggested wording. Acreages come from the reworked analysis (see [methodology.md](methodology.md)).

**Verdicts.** **Correct**: matches the source. **Correct, cite it**: right, but the deck's citation is not the source of the rule. **Partly right**: true as far as it goes, but missing something that changes the point. **Not law**: the deck presents it as a rule, but no rule was found; it is a landowner decision or a modeling choice. **Wrong**: contradicted by the source. **Unverified**: the source could not be retrieved independently.

## Bottom line

- **The answer on slide 4 holds; the reason for it shifts.** Firearms get 0 acres in Cambridge. State law (MGL c.269 §12E, no discharge within 500 ft of *any* building in use) already erases 99% of the city's dry land. The 39 acres that survive are all City or DCR parkland: Cambridge Cemetery, Fresh Pond Reservation, the municipal golf course, Kingsley Park, Alewife Brook Reservation, Magazine Beach. The citywide ban covers the same 39 acres the parkland assumption does.
- **The "plot twist" ordinance adds nothing.** If §9.16.060 bans bows only in streets and on bridges, state law already covers them: §58's 150 ft buffer around every paved road takes in every street and bridge deck. The ordinance removes 0.00 acres at 500 ft and 0.01 acre at 250 ft.
- **On Thanksgiving it's bows or nothing, everywhere in Massachusetts.** Thursday, November 26, 2026 falls in the archery-only part of the fall turkey season. Shotguns are legal only October 19–31.
- **Three of the six layers on slide 2 aren't law.** Parkland is the owner's call, permission is required only on posted land, and no rule keeps hunters away from a water supply. The parkland layer does the most work of any assumption: at 500 ft it removes 113 of the 133 acres state law leaves.
- **Three Cambridge citations are unverified.** §9.16.010, §9.16.060 and §9.20.010 couldn't be retrieved here, because Municode refuses automated access. Check them against the current code before the talk.

### 1. Citywide firearm ban: unverified, plausible

The notes cite §9.16.010. Its text couldn't be retrieved: library.municode.com returns 403 to automated requests, and no other copy turned up. Massachusetts towns can ban discharge outright. The SJC upheld a town discharge bylaw against a challenge that state hunting law preempted it (*Town of Amherst v. Attorney General*, 1986), and Medfield, for example, bans discharge of firearms and bows town-wide. So the claim is plausible, but check its exact wording and exceptions against the current code.

What the analysis shows: the ban removes nothing that state law plus the parkland assumption hadn't already removed (see #9).

### 2. Dwelling setback: correct, cite the statute

The rule is statutory. MGL c.131 §58 bars anyone from hunting "by any means on the land of another within five hundred feet of any dwelling in use, except as authorized by the owner or occupant thereof". [malegislature.gov](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXIX/Chapter131/Section58)

- It covers every method of hunting, not only archery.
- It applies "on the land of another".
- The dwelling's owner or occupant can waive it, so a neighbor's written OK opens land inside the ring.

**Suggested citation:** MGL c.131 §58 rather than [2]. Reference [2] is the page about the proposed change.

### 3. "Rules set annually by the Fisheries and Wildlife Board, 321 CMR 3.00": partly right

The Board sets seasons, bag limits and zones in 321 CMR 3.00, and it revises them yearly. The setbacks are in statute (c.131 §58), which is why cutting the archery setback to 250 ft needs the Legislature rather than the Board.

**Suggested footer:** "Seasons and bag limits are set by the Fisheries and Wildlife Board (321 CMR 3.00). Setbacks are statute: 500 ft from a dwelling in use (MGL c.131 §58). A bill pending since 2026 would cut the archery setback to 250 ft."

### 4. The 250 ft bill: correct, needs status

- **Filed:** the Governor filed the proposal in April 2026, after MassWildlife's March 2026 recommendations (11,077 public comments, 70% in support).
- **The package:** cut the archery setback from 500 ft to 250 ft "except with landowner permission", lift the statutory Sunday hunting ban and lift crossbow restrictions. ([mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/updating-hunting-laws))
- **Where it stands:** the House inserted the hunting language into S.3064, the environmental bond bill, on June 17, 2026, by 151–0. The Senate non-concurred on June 23. A conference committee was appointed on July 1. The [bill history](https://malegislature.gov/Bills/194/S3064) shows no action since then as of November 20, 2026.
- **Deadline:** bills already in conference can still be acted on until the informal session ends on January 5, 2027.

**Suggested wording:** "a pending bill would cut it to 250 ft". If the conference report passes before the talk, update this line.

### 5. "Not on public parkland": not law

No statute bars hunting on public land; the public owner decides.

- **City parks:** the City, as owner, can refuse. A Cambridge park ordinance banning hunting or weapons would make this a real local rule, but none turned up (Municode again).
- **Fresh Pond Reservation:** the Water Board's rules for the reservation don't mention hunting or weapons.
- **DCR land:** hunting is permitted on DCR property by default, except on properties listed in 302 CMR 12.11(3)–(4). No listing for the Charles River Basin or Alewife Brook Reservation turned up. DCR rules separately bar shooting within 500 ft of picnic areas, campsites, parking lots, residences and swimming areas.

**In the analysis:** this is now labeled "Not law: landowner", and it is the biggest single judgment call. Under state law alone an archer has 133 acres at 500 ft. 113 of them are public parkland: Cambridge Cemetery, Fresh Pond Reservation, Alewife Brook Reservation, the golf course, Magazine Beach and Kingsley Park.

**Suggested wording:** "Public parks: the City and DCR decide".

### 6. "Not near roads": correct, cite the statute

MGL c.131 §58 bars discharging a firearm or releasing an arrow "upon or across any state or hard surfaced highway, or within one hundred and fifty feet of any such highway". The rule covers paved roads only; the "across" clause limits the direction of a shot, which a map of where you stand doesn't capture.

**Suggested wording:** "150 ft from paved roads (MGL c.131 §58)".

### 7. "Only with landowner permission": wrong as stated

MGL c.131 §36 makes hunting on private land without permission an offense only after the owner or tenant has "conspicuously posted" notices against it. [malegislature.gov](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartI/TitleXIX/Chapter131/Section36) MassWildlife's guidance says the same: barring a town bylaw, unposted private land needs no permission.

The exceptions:
- **Inside the 500 ft ring:** you need the dwelling owner's or occupant's OK (§58).
- **Town bylaws:** some towns require written permission. Whether Cambridge does is unverified.
- **Posted land:** permission is required.

On a city lot, asking first is the only sensible course. The analysis never erased by permission; it tags each acre with its owner, which still holds.

**Suggested wording:** "Landowner's OK: required where posted (MGL c.131 §36). Ask anyway."

### 8. "Not near the water supply": not law

- **No hunting rule:** no statute or regulation was found that keeps hunters a set distance from a drinking-water supply.
- **Zone A is a land-use definition:** the 400 ft MassDEP Zone A the analysis borrowed (310 CMR 22.02) governs land use, not hunting.
- **DCR permits hunting on watershed land:** it issues hunting access permits for the Quabbin, Wachusett and Ware River watershed lands.
- **Fresh Pond:** the reservation's own rules are silent on hunting.

**Analysis change:** the Fresh Pond 400 ft buffer and the statewide Zone A exclusion are both off by default; a config flag brings either back.
- **Cambridge:** +0.15 acre at 250 ft, 0 at 500 ft. Fresh Pond Reservation is parkland anyway.
- **Statewide:** +90,000 acres at 500 ft, almost all in the Quabbin, Wachusett and Ware River watersheds.

**Suggested slide 2 row:** "Not on open water: Water features: Erase". This is physical rather than legal; you need somewhere to stand.

### 9. "Layer one did most\* of the work": answer correct, reason partly right

The 0 acres for firearms is right. But the state's own building setback (c.269 §12E) does nearly all of the work; the city ban doesn't need to.

| Step (firearm) | Acres left |
|---|---:|
| Cambridge city limits | 4,561 |
| − open water | 4,040 |
| − 500 ft from any building in use (c.269 §12E) | 53 |
| − 150 ft from paved roads (c.131 §58) | 39.5 |
| − public parkland (owner says no) | 0 |
| − citywide firearm ban (Cambridge, on top) | 0 |

The 39.5 acres state law would leave are Cambridge Cemetery (11.3), Fresh Pond Reservation (15.2 in two pieces), the municipal golf course (5.3), Kingsley Park (3.5), Alewife Brook Reservation (2.4), Charles River Basin (1.2) and Magazine Beach (0.4). All are City or DCR land ([map](../outputs/map_firearm_law.png)). The City says no twice: as landowner and by ordinance.

Separately, a shotgun isn't legal for turkey on Thanksgiving Day anywhere in the state (see [Not claimed](#not-claimed-but-it-matters-for-a-thanksgiving-turkey)).

**Suggested beat:** "State law alone leaves 39 acres for a shotgun, and every one is a City park, the City cemetery or the City golf course. Cambridge said no twice."

### 10. §9.16.060, bows only in streets and on bridges: unverified; adds nothing if true

The text couldn't be retrieved (Municode, as in #1). If the notes have it right, the ordinance is fully covered by state law. Every paved street and bridge deck is already inside §58's 150 ft buffer, so the ordinance removes **0.00 acres at 500 ft and 0.01 acre at 250 ft**. "Those five layers are back on" is right. The twist is that the city's bow rule never bites.

### 11. "Parks Commissioner": wrong

Cambridge has no Parks Commissioner. Who actually decides:
- **City parks:** the Department of Public Works (Parks Division, under the Commissioner of Public Works) maintains and operates them.
- **Fresh Pond Reservation:** the Water Department and the Water Board.
- **State reservations:** DCR runs the Charles River Basin, Alewife Brook Reservation and Magazine Beach.

**Suggested slide 5 wording:** "Decision authority: landowners, the City (DPW, Water Dept.), DCR, MassWildlife". Apply the same change to the notes' "formal report for the Cambridge Parks Commissioner".

### 12. $50 maximum fine (§9.20.010): unverified

The text couldn't be retrieved. Check it against the current code.

### 13. c.269 §12E penalty: correct

The statute sets a fine of $50 to $100, up to 3 months in a jail or house of correction, or both, for discharging a firearm within 500 ft of a dwelling or other building in use. The owner's or legal occupant's consent exempts it. It covers firearms only, not bows. [malegislature.gov](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIV/TitleI/Chapter269/Section12E)

### 14. 1871 ordinance, fifty rods: correct

Clarke, *The Charter and Ordinances of the City of Cambridge* (1871), §30. It barred discharging "any gun, pistol, or other firearm" in the principal inhabited parts of the city, or "within fifty rods of any dwelling-house therein", except in performance of a legal duty. Fifty rods × 16.5 ft = 825 ft. [Duke Center for Firearms Law](https://firearmslaw.duke.edu/laws/augustus-peck-clarke-the-charter-and-ordinances-of-the-city-of-cambridge-page-120-image-134-1871-available-at-the-making-of-modern-law-primary-sources)

## Not claimed, but it matters for a Thanksgiving turkey

- **Season.** Fall turkey season 2026 in Zones 1–13 runs October 5 to November 28. It is archery-only October 5–17 and November 2–28; shotguns and muzzleloaders are allowed October 19–31 only. Thanksgiving, Thursday November 26, is archery-only. Cambridge sits in Zone 10 (northeastern Massachusetts, from the New Hampshire line south to Wrentham and Hull), going by the zone's boundary description. The bag limit is one bird of either sex. You need a hunting license and a turkey permit, and secondary sources say an archery stamp in archery-only periods. Hours run from half an hour before sunrise to half an hour after sunset.
- **Sunday.** Every Sunday is closed (MGL c.131 §57), including carrying a bow. Thanksgiving is a Thursday, so this doesn't bite.
- **School and college grounds.** MGL c.269 §10(j) bars carrying a firearm "or other dangerous weapon" on the grounds of any school, college or university without written authorization from the board or officer in charge. The penalty is up to $1,000, 2 years, or both. Whether a bow counts as a dangerous weapon is unsettled, but two candidates sit on school land: the BB&N Athletic Fields (5.3 acres at 500 ft, 12.2 at 250 ft) and MIT's Briggs Field (250 ft). Get the school's written authorization, which you'd need as the landowner's OK anyway.
- **Crossbows** are restricted by statute, with the same pending bill. The analysis assumes a vertical bow.
